    What you are looking at

    Your phone has one speaker and one microphone, so it measures range — and nothing else. An echo at 2.4 m is somewhere on a cone tens of degrees wide, which is why every return is drawn as an arc rather than a dot. Range is accurate to a few centimetres; bearing at 3 m is blurry by more than a metre.

    That is also why you have to sweep. One ping cannot locate anything. The map sharpens where arcs from different directions cross, which is roughly what a bat gets from turning its head — and the red disc in the middle is the blind zone, where the outgoing pulse drowns everything out.

    Honest limits: about 0.6 m to 4–5 m against hard flat surfaces, less against soft ones. This is a short-range acoustic scanner, not a depth camera, and it will not see a black cat on a carpet.
